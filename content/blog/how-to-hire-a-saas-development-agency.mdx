Choosing the right engineering partner to build your SaaS is one of the highest-stakes decisions you will make as a founder. Hiring the wrong agency can burn 6 months of precious runway and leave you with unmaintainable spaghetti code that has to be completely rewritten.

In this guide, we outline the exact questions and technical benchmarks founders should use to evaluate a [SaaS Development Company](/services/saas-development).

---

## 1. The 4 Critical Questions to Ask Before Signing

### Question 1: "Who owns the code, and how is IP transferred?"
Many low-cost agencies insert hidden clauses retaining proprietary rights or holding deployment keys hostage. 

**What to look for**: The agency must grant 100% intellectual property ownership from day one. You should have direct administrative access to the GitHub repository, cloud hosting accounts, and database instances.

### Question 2: "What is your primary tech stack, and why?"
Agencies that push outdated legacy frameworks (like monolithic PHP or outdated jQuery wrappers) are often optimizing for their existing junior developers rather than your platform's scalability.

**What to look for**: Modern, type-safe, cloud-native stacks such as **Next.js (App Router), TypeScript, Tailwind CSS, and PostgreSQL**. This stack guarantees high performance, server-side rendering for SEO, and easy hiring when you later bring engineers in-house.

### Question 3: "How do you scope deliverables and pricing?"
Beware of open-ended hourly billing where incentives are aligned with dragging out development cycles.

**What to look for**: Demand fixed-milestone sprints with live staging previews every two weeks. For early-stage products, explore dedicated [SaaS MVP Development](/services/saas-mvp-development) packages with clear 4–6 week delivery deadlines.

### Question 4: "How do you handle technical debt and security?"
Ask how the team structures multi-tenant database isolation, data validation schemas, and automated CI/CD pipelines.

**What to look for**: Implementation of Row-Level Security (RLS), Zod validation across all API routes, and clean containerization via [Linux VPS / Docker deployments](/services/vps-deployment).

## 2. Red Flags When Vetting Agencies

| Warning Sign | Why It's Dangerous | What You Should Demand |
|---|---|---|
| **No Staging Previews** | You only see code after 4 months when it's too late to pivot | Live staging URL updated every Friday |
| **Open-Ended Hourly Rates** | Misaligned incentives to bill maximum hours | Transparent fixed milestone pricing |
| **No Automated Testing** | Every feature update risks breaking payments | Unit and integration testing suites |
| **Proprietary Boilerplates** | Vendor lock-in prevents you from hiring internal devs | Standard open-source TypeScript patterns |
